## Q&A cheat sheet (answer in 15–25 seconds, then stop)

Jury criteria: technical functionality & reproducibility · UX · agentic depth · originality · market potential.

**"How do you know it works?"**
A frozen 300-ticket set, dataset hash recorded. 50 are handwritten and independent: 47/50 right service,
50/50 work type. The other 250 were generated by the same model, so we treat those as optimistic.
Every run records model, prompt, KB and policy versions, and LLM calls are cached, so reruns reproduce.

**"Why not train a classifier on the 20,000 tickets?"**
They are templated: only 173 distinct descriptions and 79 comment texts. A classifier learns the
templates, not the language of real tickets. So the training data becomes our catalog: service → team
map, 21 real resolution patterns, service cards. The LLM does the reading, with retrieval over that catalog.

**"What's agentic about it?"**
It decides how much autonomy it has. Each run lands in a lane: auto-applied, needs review, or human only.
Highest priority on a critical service, any cancellation, or a weak match always goes to a human.
Autonomy is a versioned policy, per field and per service. When a new run disagrees with a human
override, it raises a conflict instead of overwriting. Closures in Jira flow back into the Core.

**"How do you stop hallucinated resolution comments?"**
Comments are drafts. Code attributes each phrase to the ticket, a historical pattern, or generation,
and flags specifics found in neither: codes, numbers, times, names. A comment that invents a ticket ID
is regenerated. The operator approves or edits before anything is written.

**"Where does confidence come from?"**
Observable signals: self-consistency across samples, retrieval margin, and agreement between the model's
service and the best-matching service card. Never the model rating itself.

**"Data privacy? Swiss context?"**
The engine is model-agnostic: Apertus via Swisscom, GPT-6 Luna, or fully local through Ollama on a Mac mini.
The playground compares models on the same tickets as shadow runs, without touching Jira.
The AI core never calls Jira itself; only the gateway does, and operators can sign in with Atlassian
so writes carry their own identity.

**"What's weak?"**
Urgency and impact: about 75% and 69% exact on the 300 set. They're judgment calls, and the labels are soft too.
Priority is always consistent with the matrix, even when urgency is debatable.
Next: learn from operator overrides through human-approved KB versions. The knowledge base never updates itself.

**"Who buys this?"**
Any Jira Service Management team with L2 triage. Start suggest-only, then turn on autonomy
service by service as agreement with operators is measured. The dashboard already tracks that agreement.

**"Speed?"**
GPT-6 Luna: 69 tickets in 72 seconds with three consistency samples, evidence and draft comments.
Apertus 70B: about 14 seconds per ticket.
